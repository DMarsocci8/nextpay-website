Shift4 discovery call script

SDR discovery call template — work through it live. Teal blocks are what you say; italics are coaching cues. You can introduce yourself as NextPay, Shift4, or SkyTab (the old name).

Friendly introduction

"Hi [Merchant's Name], this is [Your Name] from NextPay. How's your day going so far?" Wait for their response and acknowledge it. "I noticed you recently showed some interest in upgrading your point-of-sale system through one of our ads. I wanted to take a few minutes to better understand your current setup and see if we might be able to help improve your operations and save you some money. Does that sound good?"

Discovery questions

"Great! Let's dive in. I just have a few quick questions to get a better picture of your current setup."
  • Current POS hardware: "Do you currently have cutting-edge POS hardware, or an older system? What's the name of the system you're using?"
  • Tableside ordering: "Are you quick-service or sit-down dining? Do your servers use handhelds for tableside ordering, or is everything at a central POS station? Is that something you'd like to implement?"
  • Kitchen display units: "Are you using kitchen display units to streamline front-to-back-of-house communication? Do you use remote printers?"
  • Reservations system: "Do you have an integrated reservations system? How are you taking reservations today?"
  • Gift card program: "Do you offer a gift card program to your customers?"
  • Loyalty program: "Are you running a loyalty program to keep customers coming back?"
  • Cloud-based reporting: "Are you using cloud-based reporting to track sales and key metrics? Do you have an app to manage your business?"
  • Company website: "Do you have a website that integrates with your POS for online orders or reservations?"
  • Online ordering: "Are you offering online ordering, and is it integrated with your POS?"
  • Delivery integration: "Are you partnered with delivery services like Uber Eats or DoorDash? How are those integrated today?"
  • QR code ordering & payments: "Have you implemented QR code ordering or payment options for your customers?"
  • EMV & cash advantage: "Are you taking advantage of EMV tap-to-pay terminals? Are you using a dual pricing or cash discount program to eliminate card processing fees?"

Identify pain points & opportunities

"Thanks for sharing that with me! Based on what you've mentioned, it sounds like there might be some opportunities to improve efficiency and possibly reduce costs." Based on their answers, pinpoint the key areas where SkyTab by Shift4 adds value (gaps in tableside, KDS, reservations, loyalty, online ordering, dual pricing, etc.).

Close & CTA

"I think we have some solutions that could really benefit your business — especially in [mention the specific areas they're lacking or could improve]. I'd love to set up a quick demo so you can see firsthand how everything works and how it streamlines your operations. Does [specific day/time] work to hop on a quick call with one of our specialists?" If they hesitate, offer an alternative time or ask for their preferred time. "If you're ready to move forward, I can get that demo scheduled right now and send you the details. What do you think?"
Don't forget: add your own email address on the booking form so you get notified of the appointment!

Thank you & follow-up

"Perfect, I've got you down for [confirmed date/time]. You'll receive a confirmation email shortly with all the details. Thanks again for your time today, [Merchant's Name] — looking forward to our next call!" If they don't want to schedule right away, ask if it's okay to follow up, and thank them for their time regardless.

Battlecards — Shift4 vs the competition

Quick reference for head-to-head deals. Tap a competitor for their strengths, weak spots, and how to win the deal with Shift4.

vs Toast

Bar & restaurant cloud POS that grew fast on VC funding, a 2021 IPO, a direct sales team, and aggressive marketing. Proprietary Android hardware and software with bundled payments, aimed at more complex restaurants.

Pricing: Setup + monthly + per-feature processing fees. Gets costly once a merchant adds all the modules and integrations. Add-ons are available flat-rate in the in-POS shop without sales support.

Their strengths

  • Mature cloud product — handles complex modifiers and workflows.
  • Fast development and frequent product iterations.
  • "Sticky" model — easy on-ramp, then hard to leave.
  • Strong brand awareness and marketing; cash from IPO.

Their weak spots

  • Monthly fees for third-party integrations push up total cost.
  • Base SaaS + processing, then every add-on module is monetized separately.
  • Direct-sales model = inefficient, mostly-remote install and support.

How to win with Shift4

  • End-to-end payments unlock a long list of features free of charge — both setup and monthly (contactless ordering, online ordering built in).
  • Own processing means rates by card type, which can beat a flat rate depending on the customer mix.
  • Marketplace and enhanced Lighthouse Business Manager features at no extra charge — pay only for partner services.
  • Mature reseller network with local install/training/support, plus a dedicated direct team.
vs Clover

Cloud POS software, hardware, and payment processing owned by Fiserv. A general-purpose system used by restaurants, retailers, salons, and more.

Pricing: $60–$80 per month + $9.95 per additional device. Hardware and processing rates vary widely by the sales channel it's bought through.

Their strengths

  • Great hardware and reliable payments; strong brand recognition.
  • Backed by a Fortune 500 company (Fiserv).
  • Low software prices relative to Toast; can have low processing rates depending on channel.
  • Extensive reseller network.

Their weak spots

  • Not mature or tailored for sophisticated restaurants.
  • App-market model drives up the real price — features cost extra.
  • Hardware can be expensive, and processing rates are highly variable.
  • Minimal innovation in the restaurant space.

How to win with Shift4

  • Purpose-built for restaurants — Clover is one-size-fits-all and slow to innovate in F&B.
  • Lower total cost: no app-market add-ons stacking up, and hardware is included.
  • Watch for high reseller rate schedules on Clover deals — point to the real all-in cost.
  • Roughly a third of restaurants in North America already run on Shift4.
vs Heartland

A subsidiary of Global Payments. Its restaurant POS came from the MobileBytes acquisition (now Heartland Restaurant POS). Mostly dealer-exclusive, with some direct sales through CSMs in select markets.

Pricing: Priced by terminal + monthly software; $75/month for the mobile app. Two models — SaaS + upfront, or PaaS (everything in one monthly rate).

Their strengths

  • Cheaper to bundle POS + payments together; good eGift, free integrated online ordering, native kiosk.
  • Easy setup, simplified UI; low-power Bluetooth "skip the line" and tableside QR ordering.
  • Loyalty with spend history; good for small/quick-serve; mobile EMV tableside; free open-API integrations.

Their weak spots

  • Reliance on iOS/iCloud causes problems; not commercial-grade hardware.
  • Critical features (pre-auth, offline mode, SMS, cash discount) are unavailable if you don't process with Heartland.
  • Hidden/junk fees — EMV Failover Fee and a $125/month Compliance Fee; surcharge program works poorly.
  • Frequent updates cause version drift; limited feature set; support is a pain point.

How to win with Shift4

  • All features stay unlocked on Shift4's end-to-end payments — no holding functionality hostage to processing.
  • Transparent pricing — no EMV failover or monthly compliance "junk" fees.
  • Commercial-grade hardware included with the service, plus offline mode for card payments during outages.
  • Reseller + direct support network instead of a dealer-only support chain.
vs Lightspeed

Canadian company offering cloud POS, eCommerce, payments, accounting, and multi-location management. Public on the NYSE and TSX.

Pricing: Essentials $29/mo · Plus $119/mo · Pro $289/mo · Enterprise at a contracted rate. Hardware is non-proprietary.

Their strengths

  • Diversified portfolio (payments, retail, restaurant, golf/resort, eCommerce) — resilient in tough markets.
  • Owns Lightspeed Payments plus other processing options — flexible on deals.
  • Flexible, non-proprietary hardware; can reuse equipment from a prior system.

Their weak spots

  • Many config options and add-ons mean a steep learning curve to set up and train.
  • Add-ons priced separately — you need a custom quote to know the real cost.
  • Offline mode (LiteServer) still can't run credit, debit, or gift cards or third-party integrations.

How to win with Shift4

  • End-to-end payments unlock features free of charge at both setup and monthly — less dependent on software/hardware.
  • Automatic offline mode queues card transactions and processes them when the connection returns.
  • Predictable service-based pricing; Marketplace and Lighthouse Business Manager included.
  • Focused investment in the U.S. bar & restaurant market.
vs Revel

An iPad-based POS and cloud pioneer. Since 2018 it focuses on multi-location quick-serve restaurants (QSR) and no longer targets single-location merchants — a fit for established, multi-channel operations.

Pricing: Essentials/Enterprise contracted; Advantage is flat-rate processing. POS terminal $99/mo (two-terminal minimum, three-year contract); implementation starts at $674.

Their strengths

  • Strong enterprise management — deep configuration that scales as the merchant grows.
  • Multi-channel: drive-thru, delivery, and online ordering with order tracking and customer texts.
  • Various integrations available.

Their weak spots

  • High cost — customizable features require custom quotes.
  • Best pricing needs a three-year agreement and a two-terminal minimum.
  • Everything is SaaS (KDS, CDS, online ordering) — hundreds of dollars in monthly fees.
  • iOS-only hardware.

How to win with Shift4

  • The Shift4 ecosystem (Mobile, Online, Glass, CDS, KDS) lets merchants grow into features over time.
  • End-to-end payments include features free of charge instead of stacking SaaS fees.
  • Standard service pricing — end the contract as needed; custom enterprise agreements available.
  • Expanding Android hardware options, not iOS-locked.
vs SpotOn

A mix-and-match restaurant POS covering sales/inventory, analytics, and online ordering, with paid add-ons (loyalty, reservations) to build out a personalized system.

Pricing: POS with processing $25/mo · without processing $195/mo · Restaurant $65/mo + hardware · Poynt $15/mo + setup · Loyalty $65/mo · Website $65/mo. Processing 1.99% + $0.20 (2.99% + $0.20 if not on their payments).

Their strengths

  • Easy, restaurant-specific platform with detailed modifiers and web-based access.
  • Marketing and website-building tools, loyalty, and a wide range of reporting.
  • 24/7 chat, phone, and email support; low processing at 1.99% + $0.20.

Their weak spots

  • Pushes its own touchscreen hardware with a monthly fee.
  • No standard pricing — every deal needs a quote.
  • Add-on fees (loyalty, etc.) stack up quickly.
  • Processing gets expensive (2.99% + $0.20) if the merchant isn't on SpotOn payments.

How to win with Shift4

  • Lighthouse Business Manager + Marketplace include free Shift4 features — Online, QR, loyalty, email marketing, reputation, and social.
  • Hardware is free with the POS service rather than a monthly hardware fee.
  • Competitive end-to-end payments with straightforward, predictable monthly costs.
  • Custom enterprise agreements available.
vs Square

A mobile payments company offering business software, POS systems, and payment hardware. Its free base POS and no fixed monthly cost create a very low barrier to entry; the brand was built on retail mobile card readers.

Pricing: Free $0 / 2.5% + $0.10 · Plus $60 / 2.9% + $0.30 · Premium at a contracted rate.

Their strengths

  • Free entry-level option — very low commitment, with room to grow into paid tiers.
  • Extremely easy to set up and use with minimal hardware.
  • Strong brand awareness backed by traditional advertising.

Their weak spots

  • Not practical for multi-channel or multi-location restaurants — lacks advanced modifiers and channels.
  • Built for iOS with limited hardware and KDS options.
  • Relies heavily on remote, merchant self-installation — less on-site support.

How to win with Shift4

  • End-to-end payments include features and hardware free of charge.
  • Built for food & beverage — advanced modifiers and integrations that grow with the merchant across locations.
  • Mature reseller network plus a direct team to install, train, and support — not self-install.
  • Expanding Android hardware options; Shift4 brand visibility (SpaceX, St. Jude partnerships).
vs Tabit

An Israeli-founded, mobile-first iPad POS now headquartered in Aventura, FL. Payment-agnostic with no direct sales — its strategy is to convert resellers with partner programs that let them keep payments revenue.

Pricing: Variable dealer-to-dealer (some bundle networking gear, which obscures the price). Generally about $49/month per mobile device.

Their strengths

  • Mobile-first — every device is a full POS station, speeding kitchen communication.
  • Cloud-based central database linking online ordering, reservations, and more.
  • Reseller-focused partner programs; rich native ecosystem (ordering, delivery, reservations, BI, KDS).

Their weak spots

  • No offline mode yet — needs a redundant WAN connection at extra cost or transactions stop.
  • Inconsistent experience — no direct implementation/support and no remote support.
  • Clunky back end, complex setup, and heavy partner training required.

How to win with Shift4

  • Shift4 POS is also cloud-based, with a robust integrated ecosystem that includes payments.
  • Offline mode keeps processing card payments during an outage — no redundant-WAN workaround.
  • Support agents can remote into terminals; intuitive back office and on-POS configuration.
  • Reseller + direct support model for consistent install, training, and service.

About SkyTab & Shift4

Know the product and the company behind it — it's a strong brand to sell against.

The product — SkyTab

SkyTab is Shift4's purpose-built restaurant POS ecosystem — countertop stations, SkyTab Glass tablets, SkyTab Mobile/Air handhelds, kitchen display systems, customer-facing displays and self-order kiosks — all tied to end-to-end Shift4 payments.

  • Built for restaurants & bars — modifiers, coursing, tabs, tableside ordering and pay-at-table.
  • Included, not add-ons — online ordering, QR order & pay, loyalty & gift, reputation and email/SMS marketing come with the platform.
  • Hardware included with the POS service, with a lifetime warranty.
  • True offline mode — keep taking orders and card payments if the internet drops, then sync when it's back.
  • Business manager back office (Lighthouse / Customer Hub) — real-time reporting and marketing from anywhere.

The company — Shift4

Shift4 Payments (NYSE: FOUR) is a large U.S. payments-technology company providing integrated payments and commerce tech across restaurants, hospitality, stadiums and venues, and more.

  • Deep restaurant footprint — by Shift4's own numbers, they serve roughly a third of North American restaurants.
  • End-to-end payments — because Shift4 owns the processing, it can bundle features rivals monetize separately, and its Advantage (dual-pricing) program can take card processing toward 0% for the merchant.
  • Well-capitalized, growing brand — public company with high-profile clients and sponsorships and a strong hospitality-tech roadmap.
  • Real support — a mature reseller network for local install, training and service, plus a direct SkyTab team and 24/7 support.

Use these as talking points; confirm current figures with Shift4 before quoting specifics to a merchant.
