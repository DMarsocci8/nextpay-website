Who they are: Toast is a bar & restaurant cloud POS that grew fast on VC money, an IPO and a big direct sales team. Proprietary Android hardware, bundled Toast Payments, and a huge catalog of add-on modules (online ordering, loyalty, payroll, xtraCHEF inventory, delivery).
Their pricing: setup + monthly SaaS + processing, and every add-on module is billed separately — costs stack fast for a fully integrated restaurant, plus monthly fees for third-party integrations.

Where Toast is strong

  • Mature cloud product — handles complex menus and modifiers, ships new features quickly.
  • Sticky ecosystem: their own online ordering, delivery, analytics and payroll keep merchants on the hook.
  • Big brand awareness — social media, merchant-facing content, community.
  • IPO cash funds an aggressive direct sales team.

Where Toast is weak

  • Monthly fees for third-party integrations drive the real cost way up — they need those fees to fund growth.
  • Base product is SaaS + processing, then each module is monetized separately — the quote never matches the final bill.
  • Direct sales model means most install and support work is done remotely — no local presence.
  • Multi-year contracts with early-termination fees.

How you win with SkyTab by Shift4

  • End-to-end pricing: online ordering, QR order & pay, contactless payments and more are included free — no setup, no monthly — where Toast bills each as a line item.
  • Customer Hub (formerly Lighthouse) business tools and the Marketplace are included, not upsold.
  • Local install, training and support through the reseller network — not a remote ticket queue.
  • No multi-year lock-in, and a $5,000 switch incentive to offset leaving Toast.

What to say: "You can get the same restaurant toolkit — KDS, online ordering, handhelds, offline mode — without the multi-year contract or Toast's stacking module fees. And we'll put $5,000 toward your switch."

They'll say → you say

"Toast is built for restaurants."
"So is SkyTab by Shift4 — a third of restaurants in North America run on Shift4. Same tools, but online ordering and QR pay are included instead of billed as separate modules."
"We already invested in Toast."
"That's exactly what the $5,000 switch incentive is for. Let's add up your Toast bill with every module and integration fee on it — then compare."

Who they are: Clover is Fiserv's cloud POS — good hardware, big brand, huge reseller network, used by everyone from barber shops to restaurants.
Their pricing: roughly $60–80/mo + $9.95 per additional device on restaurant plans — but the real cost climbs through the App Market, and processing rates vary wildly depending on who sold the account.

Where Clover is strong

  • Great hardware and reliable payments.
  • Brand recognition, backed by a Fortune 500 company (Fiserv).
  • Software cheaper than Toast; processing can be low depending on the sales channel.
  • Extensive reseller network.

Where Clover is weak

  • One-size-fits-all — not mature or tailored for sophisticated restaurants.
  • App-market model: the "low" software price balloons once you add the apps a restaurant actually needs.
  • Hardware is expensive — and when a reseller subsidizes it, the processing rate is usually padded to pay for it.
  • Highly variable processing rates and minimal restaurant innovation.

How you win with SkyTab by Shift4

  • Purpose-built for restaurants — richer features out of the box, regular updates, no app market to nickel-and-dime.
  • Hardware is more affordable and included in the cost — lower total cost of ownership.
  • Shift4 runs a third of North American restaurants — this isn't a niche product.
  • If they're on Clover already, pull the statement — reseller accounts often sit on high rate schedules. That's your opening.
Heads up: we sell Clover too. This card is for restaurant deals where Clover is the wrong fit — if Clover genuinely fits (retail, services, simple counter service), place Clover through us and win on rate and service instead.

What to say: "Clover is a general-purpose POS wearing a restaurant costume. SkyTab by Shift4 is built only for restaurants — and once you add up Clover's apps and the rate you're actually on, we're the cheaper system too."

They'll say → you say

"Clover's monthly price is lower."
"The sticker is — the total isn't. KDS, online ordering, loyalty all come from the app market at a monthly fee each. With SkyTab by Shift4 that's built in."
"My Clover was basically free."
"Subsidized hardware gets paid back somewhere — usually in the processing rate. Bring me a statement and I'll show you what that 'free' Clover really costs a month."

Who they are: Square offers a free base-level POS with no fixed monthly cost — an incredible value and a very low barrier to entry, which is why so many small restaurants start there. iPad/iOS-based hardware.
Their pricing: Free plan at $0/mo; Plus around $60/mo; Premium is a contracted rate — plus flat-rate processing on every card.

Where Square is strong

  • Free entry-level plan — very low commitment for a new merchant, with paid tiers to grow into.
  • Very easy to set up and use; simple interface, minimal hardware.

Where Square is weak

  • Not practical for multi-channel or multi-location — lacks the modifier depth and channel handling bars and restaurants need.
  • Easy to start with, but merchants outgrow it as they expand.
  • Limited, iOS-built hardware — thin options for KDS and back-of-house.
  • Relies on remote implementation and merchant self-install — little live, on-site support.

How you win with SkyTab by Shift4

  • Real hardware options on commercial-grade Android — stations, handhelds, KDS.
  • End-to-end pricing opens up features, hardware and functionality free of charge.
  • Local resellers install, train and support the merchant in person.
Heads up: we sell Square too. This card is for restaurants that have outgrown it — for a simple counter-service or retail merchant, placing Square through us can be the right call.

What to say: "Square got you open — SkyTab by Shift4 keeps you growing. When you're juggling modifiers, online orders and a kitchen, you need a system built for restaurants and a person who shows up, not a help center."

  • Easy-to-use, restaurant-specific platform — detailed modifiers, separate menu types, web-based management.

Where SpotOn is weak

  • Monthly fees for the hardware itself.
  • No standard pricing — you can't know the cost without a custom quote.
  • Add-ons stack fast: $65 loyalty here, $65 website there — the "cheap POS" isn't cheap by the end.
  • Leave their processing and the rate jumps a full point to 2.99% + 20¢ — that low rate is a leash.

How you win with SkyTab by Shift4

  • Hardware is free with the POS service — no monthly hardware rental.
  • Straightforward monthly costs — no quote required to know what you'll pay.
  • Loyalty, email marketing, reputation management, social tools and third-party integrations are free through Customer Hub and the Marketplace — SpotOn charges for each.
  • Free of charge setup and monthly on features that SpotOn line-items.

What to say: "SpotOn quotes you a $25 POS, then adds $65 for loyalty, $65 for the website and a monthly fee for the hardware. With us the hardware's free, the tools are included, and the price is the price."

Who they are: Heartland (a Global Payments subsidiary) sells Heartland Restaurant POS — the former MobileBytes — an iPad/iOS system sold mostly through dealers.
Their pricing: priced per terminal + monthly software (mobile app $75/mo), sold either as SaaS + upfront hardware or as one monthly PaaS rate — plus a $125/mo compliance fee and payment "junk fees" like an EMV failover fee.

Where Heartland is strong

  • Cheaper when POS and payments are bundled together.
  • Good eGift program, free integrated online ordering, native kiosk app.
  • Easy to set up; tableside QR ordering and loyalty features.
  • Decent enterprise menu capabilities and free open-API integrations.

Where Heartland is weak

  • Consumer iPads, not commercial-grade hardware — and iOS/iCloud reliance causes real problems.
  • If you don't process with Heartland, critical features shut off: pre-auth, offline mode, SMS, cash discount and more.
  • Hidden fees: EMV failover fee, $125/mo compliance fee, payment junk fees.
  • Surcharge program adds the fee after the total is shown — confusing guests — and support is a nightmare.
  • KDS needs an iPad, has no reporting; dealer-dependent install quality.

How you win with SkyTab by Shift4

  • Commercial-grade Android hardware built for a greasy, busy line — not a consumer iPad.
  • Our dual-pricing / cash-discount programs are compliant and shown to the guest before the total — no surprise-fee confusion.
  • Advanced KDS (rush, merge, list view, all-day production) with reporting.
  • Payments fully integrated — no separate payment app — and integrations set up in a couple of clicks in the Marketplace.
  • Pull their statement — the junk fees and $125/mo compliance fee are your closing argument.

What to say: "Check your Heartland statement for the compliance fee and the EMV failover fee — that's real money every month for nothing. We run commercial hardware, compliant dual pricing, and no junk fees."

They'll say → you say

Who they are: Lightspeed is a Canadian public company with cloud POS across retail, restaurant, golf and eCommerce. Restaurant runs on the L-Series with non-proprietary hardware.
Their pricing: Essentials $29/mo, Plus $119/mo, Pro $289/mo, Enterprise contracted — with add-ons priced separately, so you need a custom quote to know the real cost.

Where Lightspeed is strong

  • Diversified portfolio (retail, restaurant, golf, eCom) keeps them resilient.
  • Own payment processing plus multiple processor options — flexible on the payments side.
  • Non-proprietary hardware — merchants can sometimes reuse existing gear.

Where Lightspeed is weak

  • A maze of configuration options, add-ons and integrations — steep learning curve for setup and staff training.
  • Add-on pricing means the sticker price is fiction — you need a custom quote to know the cost.
  • Offline is the killer: without their LiteServer, an internet drop means no credit, debit or gift cards — and even with it, integrations and reporting stop.

How you win with SkyTab by Shift4

  • True offline mode: devices switch to offline automatically when an authorization fails, queue the transactions, and process when the connection returns — cards keep working when the internet doesn't.
  • Straightforward, service-based pricing — predictable, no quote roulette.
  • Customer Hub and Marketplace features included at no additional charge.
  • Local dealers handle install and training, so the "learning curve" is our problem, not the merchant's.

What to say: "Ask Lightspeed what happens to card payments when the internet drops. With SkyTab by Shift4 the terminal flips to offline mode and keeps taking cards — that alone can pay for the switch on one busy Friday."

They'll say → you say

  • Intuitive back office, with most settings manageable right from the POS — menu, employees, the works.
  • Native solutions AND Marketplace integrations — cloud-based like Tabit, without the fragility.

What to say: "Ask Tabit one question: what happens to card payments when the Wi-Fi goes down on a Saturday night? Their answer is 'buy a second internet line.' Ours is 'the terminal keeps working.'"

Why merchants use Square: dead-simple setup, a free POS app, and an all-in-one ecosystem — it's the easy default for new and small businesses.

Where Square falls short

  • Flat fees that get expensive at volume (~2.6% + 10¢ in person, 2.9% + 30¢ online) — and there's no rate to negotiate.
  • Account holds, reserves and sudden deactivations are a well-known risk — your deposits can freeze with little warning.
  • Support is mostly self-serve; no dedicated rep when something breaks.
  • No real dual-pricing/surcharge program to eliminate fees the way we do.
  • You're locked to Square's processing — you can't shop the rate.

How NextPay wins

  • Match or beat the rate, and dual pricing can take processing to near zero.
  • Brand-agnostic hardware — you're not locked into one ecosystem.
  • A dedicated specialist on your account, not a help center.
  • Stable banking relationships — no surprise holds on your money.
  • Keep the simple checkout feel, lose the fees.

What to say: "Keep everything you like about Square — lose the fees and the held funds. Same easy checkout, a real rate we can match or beat, and an actual person on your account."

They'll say → you say

"Square is free."
"The app is — the processing isn't. That 2.6% adds up fast, and with dual pricing we can take it close to zero."
"I've never had a problem with it."
"Most people don't, until they do — a hold on your deposits at the wrong time can be brutal. We give you stability and a rep to call."

Why merchants use Stripe: developer-first with best-in-class APIs — a favorite for online, SaaS and marketplace businesses.

Where Stripe falls short

  • 2.9% + 30¢ flat with little room to negotiate at the SMB level.
  • Ticket-based support, and account freezes/holds are common.
  • Overkill for main-street and in-person businesses; in-person Terminal is clunky for non-developers.
  • You shoulder a lot of the compliance and dispute work yourself.
  • Limited built-in surcharging / dual pricing for everyday merchants.

How NextPay wins

  • Modern gateways — FluidPay, NMI, Authorize.net — with similar capabilities at a lower, negotiated rate.
  • Dual pricing / surcharge built in to cut fees.
  • A real human for setup, disputes and support.
  • Better fit for businesses that also take in-person payments.

What to say: "If you're not a software company, you're paying Stripe's premium for tools you don't need. We give you the gateway power, a lower rate, and real support."
