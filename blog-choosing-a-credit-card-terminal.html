Credit card terminal buying guide: PAX, Dejavoo, Clover & Valor

Countertop, wireless or handheld — how the major terminal lines differ and which one fits your counter.

If a full POS is more than you need, a modern card terminal does the job for a fraction of the cost — and today's terminals are little Android computers with receipt printers, not the beige bricks of 2010. Here's how the lines we carry differ.

PAX — the Android flagship line

PAX's A-series (A920 Pro and family) is the most popular smart terminal on the market for a reason: sharp touchscreen, fast printer, all-day battery, and an app marketplace. It handles tap, dip, swipe, QR and digital wallets, and runs dual pricing with compliant receipts. If you want one device that does everything at the counter or on the floor, start here.

Dejavoo — the value workhorses

Dejavoo's P-line and QD-line cover countertop and wireless at aggressive price points, and they pair natively with the iPOSPays gateway — which means clean dual pricing in-store and online from one ecosystem. A favorite for convenience stores, delis and service counters.

Clover Flex & Go — polish and portability

Clover Flex is a handheld POS-terminal hybrid: orders, payments, receipts and apps in one device. Clover Go clips card acceptance onto a phone. Both make sense when you want Clover's software experience without a full station.

Valor VL — dual pricing done right

Valor built its terminals around fee programs from day one — dual pricing displays, compliant receipts, and The Vault + PayNow links tying the counter to online payments.

Picking in three questions

  • Where do you take payments? Counter only → countertop. Tables, lines or job sites → wireless/handheld.
  • Running a fee program? All four lines support dual pricing — we configure it and the signage.
  • Need it to talk to software? Match the terminal to your gateway (Dejavoo→iPOSPays, Valor→Valor gateway) so payments reconcile themselves.
Terminals through NextPay come programmed, tested and drop-shipped — plug in, take a payment. No "call this 800 number to activate" purgatory.
