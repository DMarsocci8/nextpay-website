Scripts & Templates

Word-for-word scripts organized by product, plus ready-to-send text and email templates. Pick a tab, personalize the [brackets], and go. Every script follows the same flow — open, get to the decision maker, ask leading questions, close for the appointment, handle objections.

📋 New: Discovery Call Playbook — how to approach & what to ask first

Three ways to open: lead with savings (beat their current rate), rewards (earn points on sales they already make), or dual pricing (get to 0% fees — pass the card fee to the customer, compliantly). Pick the hook that fits the merchant. Get the foot in the door, then layer on the product script. [brackets] = personalize.

Cold call — rewards-first

Agent: "Hi [name], [your name] with NextPay. Quick question — are you earning any points or cash back on your business sales right now? Almost nobody is. You run thousands through every month and get nothing for it. I help owners turn that into travel and rewards — like a card, but on the money coming in. Worth two minutes?"

Why it works: you're adding value, not threatening their setup. Easy yes → gets you the statement → you find the savings too.

Walk-in / door (in person)

Agent: "Hey, I'm [your name] — I work with businesses on the block to cut card processing costs. I'm not here to sell you anything today; grab a recent statement and I'll tell you in two minutes whether I can save you money or not. Who handles that here?"

Disarm first. Ask for the statement or the owner's best time — don't pitch in the doorway.

Gatekeeper

Savings angle: "Hi, maybe you can help me — I work with businesses on lowering their card fees. Who's the best person for that, and is now an okay time to reach them?"

Rewards angle: "Hi, maybe you can help me — I help businesses earn points and cash back on the sales they already make. Who's the best person to talk to, and is now an okay time?"

Dual-pricing angle: "Hi, maybe you can help me — I help businesses get to 0% on card processing with dual pricing. Who's the best person for that, and is now an okay time?"

Friendly and brief. Treat the gatekeeper as an ally, not an obstacle.

Follow-up — no response / no-show

Savings angle: "Hi [name] — we hadn't connected, which usually means the timing's off or it slipped down the list. Totally fine either way. Should I close it out, or is it worth a quick look at lowering your fees in [month]? Either answer helps."

Rewards angle: "Hi [name] — never connected, no worries. Quick one: you're running thousands in sales a month and earning nothing back on it. Worth 10 minutes to see what you'd be earning — or should I close it out?"

Dual-pricing angle: "Hi [name] — never connected, no worries. Quick one: are you still paying card fees out of pocket? We can get you to 0% with dual pricing. Worth 10 minutes, or should I close it out?"

Negative-reverse re-engage — giving permission to say no often gets a real reply.

Inbound lead (they reached out)

"Let me think about it" — the save

Isolate the real objection instead of accepting the stall, then handle that one thing.

Price & rate

"Your rate isn't lower than what I have."
"Then I'm not the right call — and I'll tell you straight. But most owners compare the homepage rate, not their effective rate. Send me a statement; if I can't beat it, I'll say so."
"It's only a small savings."
"Fair — but it's the same money every month for years, and we can stack rewards or get you to 0% with dual pricing. Small per month, real per year."
"Are there fees to switch?"
"No switch fees on our side, and we cover onboarding, install and training. The only thing that changes is you keep more of every sale."

Happy / not interested

"We're not interested."
"Totally fair — most owners say that before they see the numbers. If I could show you found money in five minutes, worth it? If not, I'll leave you alone."
"We're happy with our processor."
"Good — keep them if you like them. The real question is whether they're paying you back in rewards or getting you to 0%. If not, you're leaving money on the table. Quick look?"
"We just signed a contract."
"Makes sense to let it settle. Let me do a free review now so you know where you stand, and I'll circle back before renewal. No pressure."

Timing & effort

"It's not a good time / too busy."
"That's exactly why I keep it to 10 minutes and do the heavy lifting. When's calmer — early morning or end of day?"
"I don't want the hassle of switching."
"We handle setup, migration and training, and you run as-is until the new one's ready. No downtime, no scramble."
"Just send me some info."
"Happy to — but a generic email won't tell you your numbers. Give me 10 minutes and I'll send something built for your business. Around [day/time]?"

Trust & skepticism

"Sounds too good to be true."
"I get it — it's not magic, it's [dual pricing / a real rewards program / interchange-plus]. Let me run your actual numbers and you decide."
"I've been burned by a processor before."
"Then you'll appreciate this: transparent pricing, no long-term contract, and a real person — me — you can call. Let me earn it on a small first step."

Dual pricing concerns

"My customers will be upset about a fee."
"Most aren't — it's a small difference, posted clearly, and plenty pay cash or debit. Shops around you already do it. We give you the signage and compliant receipts so it's done right."
"Is dual pricing even legal?"
"Yes — done correctly with proper signage and both prices shown. We set it up compliantly so you're covered."
"I don't want to lose card sales."
"You won't — people still pay by card all day; the fee just stops coming out of your pocket. We can even ease in with a smaller card-fee program."

Which standalone terminal to recommend

  • PAX (A920 / A920 Pro, A77, A800, A35): Android smart terminals — touchscreen, built-in printer, 4G + Wi-Fi. The default for most. A920 for mobile / pay-at-table, A800 for a fixed countertop.
  • Dejavoo (P-line, QD line, Z-line): Reliable countertop & handheld, pairs with the iPOSPays gateway — great when you want dual pricing dialed in.
  • SwipeSimple (B250 / B350 reader + app): Phone-based, ultra-simple — perfect for mobile, micro-merchants, or a second register.
  • Valor (VL100 / VL110 / VP500): Low-cost with dual pricing built in — the value play.
  • Ingenico (Desk 5000 / Move 5000): Classic countertop feel for owners who want familiar and simple.

Rule of thumb: lead with PAX (covers most), Dejavoo for dual-pricing-first, SwipeSimple for phone / on-the-go. The terminal is the foot in the door — win the processing, upsell a POS later only if they need it.
