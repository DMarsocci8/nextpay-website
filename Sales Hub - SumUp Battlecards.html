Quick objection handling

"Clover has more features."
They have a big app marketplace, but those features are paid add-ons from different companies — multiple bills, logins and support lines. With SumUp the core functionality is already built in and supported by one US-based team.
"Clover is cheaper."
It can look that way at first. But add up what you actually pay each month across all your apps — SumUp's all-in price usually comes in lower, and you're not managing a dozen subscriptions.
"My bank offers Clover."
A lot of banks distribute Clover — but that usually comes with a 36-month contract and pricing set by a reseller, not Clover directly. Worth looking at what you're actually locked into.
"I need QSR speed: modifiers, quick menus, kitchen support."
SumUp has all that — modifiers, KDS, fast checkout. Plus loyalty is built into checkout, so you're building your customer list every time you ring someone up.

Trap questions to ask merchants

  • Are you locked into a multi-year agreement today?
  • How many monthly fees are you paying across software, apps, and support?
  • How are you driving repeat visits today — do you have a customer list you can actually message?
  • Which features are included vs paid add-ons, and who sets the price: the bank/reseller or the POS provider?
  • Who do you call when something breaks: Clover, your bank, or the app provider?

lightspeed

Who are they? Lightspeed has separate products for retail (X-Series) and restaurants (K-Series), and targets growing, multi-location businesses with deep inventory management and analytics. Pricing starts at $109/mo for retail and $69/mo for restaurants and climbs quickly with add-ons, extra registers, and extra locations.

Top 3 reasons merchants choose SumUp
1

Built for SMB

SumUp is built for SMBs of all sizes, from 1 location to enterprise. Lightspeed's features are geared toward multi-location, so a small shop is likely paying for features it's not using.

2

Flexibility

SumUp lets partners bring their own processor. Lightspeed requires Lightspeed Payments for most retail merchants — costly for the few using an external processor.

3

Transparent Pricing

A SumUp contract means fixed monthly fees and published rates. Lightspeed doesn't publish pricing for most add-ons, so merchants don't know the real cost until they're already in a sales conversation.

Quick objection handling

"Lightspeed has better inventory management."
For a retailer managing thousands of SKUs across multiple warehouses, maybe. But as a one- or two-location SMB, SumUp's inventory does everything you need without the complexity or the price tag.
"Lightspeed has better analytics and reporting."
Their reporting is strong, but it's locked behind the Core plan at $179–$189/mo or higher. SumUp includes reporting at every tier — you know what's selling and who's coming back from day one.
"I want a system I can grow into."
Growth shouldn't mean paying for features you don't use yet. SumUp scales with you, and when you do need them, loyalty and marketing are already included.

  • How long did it take to fully set up Lightspeed and train your staff on it?

They emphasize free or heavily subsidized hardware to lock merchants into their processing contracts. NRS is known in the convenience-store and tobacco segments, but lacks the breadth of a true growth platform.

Top 3 reasons merchants choose SumUp
1

Built for Growth

SumUp is designed to bring customers back with tools that grow the business. NRS is built to ring up transactions — its marketing features are antiquated and cost extra.

2

Transparent, All-in Pricing

SumUp's SaaS fees are fixed and predictable. NRS bundles processing with hidden rate markups and long-term contracts, so merchants don't realize how much they're paying.

3

People-Powered Support

SumUp has US-based phone support for all merchants. NRS support is primarily ticket-based and often slow, leaving merchants stuck during business hours.

Quick objection handling

"The NRS point of sale is free."
It's not free; you pay for it through their processing rates. NRS locks you into NRS PAY with inflated margins. Add up what you're actually paying per swipe over 3–4 years and you'll likely spend more than if you bought your hardware outright and kept transparent processing with SumUp.
"NRS is built for stores like mine; they understand my business."
NRS has a basic platform that rings up sales and runs reports. SumUp does that AND gives you tools to grow: loyalty, automated text marketing, and a customer database. Your POS should help you bring customers back, not just check them out.
"I like that NRS integrates with my security cameras."
Camera integration is a nice perk, but it requires separate equipment and an upgraded plan. What's doing more for your bottom line: a camera overlay on your POS, or automated marketing that brings customers back every week?

Trap questions to ask merchants

  • How are you bringing customers back to your store right now? Are you using NRS loyalty, or is that on a plan you don't have?
  • How many of NRS's premium features are you actually paying for separately vs. what you thought came with the system?

shopify

Who are they? Shopify is an eCommerce-first platform that combines online store tools, POS, payments, and apps into one ecosystem. Retailers can sell in-person, online, and on social media, but brick-and-mortar businesses are required to subscribe to an eCommerce plan. Tiered processing means higher sales result in more transaction fees.

Top 3 reasons merchants choose SumUp
1

Built for In-Person SMBs

SumUp was designed for brick-and-mortar businesses, while Shopify is adapting online tools to in-person transactions. They force everyone to pay for an online store.

2

Transparent + Inclusive Pricing

SumUp only requires one software fee per location with automated marketing and inventory included. Shopify requires a base eComm plan plus POS Pro.

3

People-Powered Support

SumUp has US-based phone support for all merchants. Shopify phone support is only available to Pro merchants; Basic and Grow plan merchants are limited to chat and email.

Quick objection handling

"Shopify POS syncs online and offline inventory."
That's valuable for merchants already on Shopify's eCommerce. If most of your sales are at the counter, you're paying for an eComm subscription just to get a POS. With us, inventory syncs across locations for just $19/month.
"Shopify is an all-in-one system."
It is — for online sellers. But for a brick-and-mortar business you're largely paying to power an online store. Our platform was built for in-person businesses, so you're only paying for what you actually use.
"We have a Shopify website so it makes sense to use that POS."
That's fair, and if your online store is driving real revenue it might make sense to keep it. But you're paying your Shopify plan plus $89/mo for Pro per location, so you may be spending more on the POS than you realize — especially with marketing and loyalty, which are included with SumUp.

Trap questions to ask merchants

  • Are you actively selling online, or did you get Shopify mainly for the POS?
  • Do you know how much of your monthly Shopify bill is just for the eComm plan versus the POS?
  • How often do you call Shopify support, and how long does it take to get someone on the phone?
  • Are you using Shopify's marketing tools, or are you mostly just processing transactions?

Who are they? Square, part of global fintech Block, Inc., is a payments and software company targeting small businesses with a well-known brand and $0 entry-level plan. They offer an integrated stack: POS, online ordering, invoicing, payroll, marketing, and banking. The $0 plan makes them the default for new merchants, but the loyalty features that actually drive repeat business are gated behind paid tiers.

Top 3 reasons merchants choose SumUp
1

All-Inclusive SMS Marketing

The cost of SumUp's built-in marketing is fixed regardless of database size. Square's SMS costs scale as the customer list grows, and the service carries a monthly fee.

2

Dual Pricing / Surcharging

SumUp's POS has built-in dual pricing, so merchants can pass transaction fees to the consumer. Square cannot detect credit card charges — an important distinction for today's merchants.

3

People-Powered Support

SumUp has US-based phone support for all merchants. Lowest-tier Square merchants get phone support for just 90 days and often rely on community forums.

Quick objection handling

"Square is free to start."
It is, but the free plan doesn't include loyalty, marketing, or staff management. Once you add those, you're on Plus for $49/month, and SMS costs go up from there. With SumUp, everything is included from day one.
"Square is everywhere. Everyone knows it."
Brand recognition is real, but your customers don't come back because of who processes your payments. They come back because of reminders like our AutoPilot messaging, and Square's marketing is not as robust.
"I can always add features later with Square."
You can, but every feature bumps the bill. Loyalty requires Plus at $49/month. 24/7 phone support requires Premium at $149/month. Send more than 500 texts per month and you're paying per text. By the time you have the full toolkit, you're paying more than you would with SumUp.

  • Is Square doing anything automatically to bring customers back, or are you doing that all yourself?

SumUp vs

toast

Who are they? Toast is a restaurant-first POS platform with strong brand recognition. They sell direct to merchants with expensive proprietary hardware. They advertise a $0 Starter Kit, but SaaS fees are often much higher, and contracts usually run 2 years. Add-ons such as marketing, gift cards, payroll, and delivery are extra. Toast is expanding into retail, but the platform is still designed for restaurants. Onboarding and implementation starts at $849 and can go over $1,000 for in-person service.

Top 3 reasons merchants choose SumUp
1

All-Inclusive Growth Tools

SumUp's loyalty, SMS, and automated marketing campaigns are included in Plus and Pro tiers. Toast charges extra for all of those — the base marketing plan includes only 500 texts.

2

Hardware Costs

With SumUp, merchants buy modular hardware that they own outright. Toast requires proprietary devices that can't transfer if they move to another point of sale.

3

Transparent Pricing

A SumUp contract means fixed monthly fees and published rates. Toast doesn't publish pricing for most add-ons, so merchants don't know the real cost until they're already in a sales conversation.

Quick objection handling

"Toast is the industry standard for restaurants."
Toast is built for full-service restaurants. If the merchant runs a QSR, café, or retail shop, they're paying for a restaurant-grade system designed for someone else. SumUp is built for QSR, retail, and services in mind.
"Toast has a free plan."
It's free until you need marketing, loyalty, gift cards, or a second terminal. Once you add what actually drives repeat business, you're on a custom quote with a two-year contract. SumUp includes growth tools from day one.
"Toast has 24/7 support included."
That's true, but think about how often you actually need to call support at 3am versus how often you need loyalty and marketing tools that work automatically. SumUp includes both US-based support and the growth tools that drive repeat business.
